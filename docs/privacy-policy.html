Wockett

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 16, 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how Scoops Dev LLC, a Vermont limited liability company ("Scoops Dev", "we", "us"), the developer and publisher of Wockett ("the App"), handles your information when you use the App. We are committed to being transparent and collecting only what is necessary to make the app work.

Summary

Wockett is a local-first app. We do not operate servers of our own, do not create user accounts, and do not sell or share your personal data. Your data lives on your device and, if you use iCloud, syncs between your devices through your own private iCloud storage — where we cannot see it. The only information that ever becomes visible to other people is community content you explicitly choose to publish (see "Community content" below) and, from version 1.17, combined counts worked out from trail reports, which never show who answered or how any one person answered (see "Trail reports" below). Some features use Apple's own services (Maps, Weather, Health, iCloud) — those interactions are governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

What We Access and Why

Data Purpose Stored or shared?
Location (while using the app) Generate nearby walking route suggestions; track your position during navigation; calculate distance walked Your live position is used on-device only during active walks. Route generation and street-level previews send coordinate data to Apple's Maps services, and the elevation chart for a selected route sends that route's coordinates to OpenTopoData (see Third-Party Services) — no personal information, account, or device identifier is included with either. Scoops Dev never receives or stores your location. A GPS track may be saved temporarily to your device (see Walk Resume below).
Walk history Remember completed walks so you can view your history and repeat favourite routes Stored on your device (route name, date, distance, duration, GPS waypoints) and synced between your devices through your own private iCloud database via Apple's CloudKit. This private database belongs to your Apple Account — Scoops Dev cannot access or read it. Nothing is transmitted to us.
Walk resume Allow you to pick up a walk where you left off if the app is closed mid-session Your walk progress (including GPS track points) is temporarily saved to your device. If the walk is not resumed within 4 hours, it is automatically ended and saved to your Walk History like any completed walk (where you can delete it at any time), and the temporary checkpoint is deleted.
Local notifications Water break reminders and scheduled walk reminders Reminders are local notifications only — no server is involved. Wockett never sends you marketing or remote push notifications. Apple's iCloud service does deliver silent, invisible background signals to keep your own data in sync between your devices; these are handled entirely by Apple.
HealthKit (Apple Health) Read, with your permission: step count and walking + running distance (goal progress); walking speed, step length, walking asymmetry and double support time (the walking-steadiness and gait views); walking heart rate average, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep, active energy and flights climbed (the recovery and daily summary views). Write, with your permission: completed walks and rides as workouts, with distance, cycling distance, active energy and the GPS route. Health data is read on your device to show it to you. Summaries the App works out from it, such as streaks and badges, sync with your other Wockett data through your private iCloud (see Walk history). Health figures appear in community content only when you choose to publish them: an achievement you share to the feed, or, in versions 1.14 and 1.15, a step challenge entry. In those versions, when you join or sync a step challenge, the App adds up the steps Apple Health recorded since the challenge began and publishes that total with your entry (see Community content). From version 1.16, a step challenge total comes from the walks you recorded in Wockett, not from Apple Health. Completed walks and rides are written to Apple Health on your device. No Health data is transmitted to Scoops Dev or any third party, and it is never used for advertising. You choose which types to allow, and can change this at any time in the Health app.
Weather (via Apple WeatherKit) Display weather conditions for suggested routes Fetched by Apple's WeatherKit service for the place the weather is shown for: your current location, or a point on the route you are looking at or walking. The App sends that location as it is, without rounding it. Governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.
Community content (optional) Share routes with the community, join challenges, and post to the achievement feed Only if you choose to publish: shared route names and paths, challenge entries, and feed posts are stored in Apple's public CloudKit database and are visible to other Wockett users. In versions 1.14 and 1.15 a shared route's whole path is published, including where it starts and finishes. From version 1.16 the App publishes a shared route as drawn, including where it starts and finishes, unless you turn on Hide start and finish when you share it; both share screens warn that a route which starts or ends at your home shows where you live. With Hide start and finish on, the start and finish are hidden inside a privacy area of at least 200 metres, whose centre is moved by a private random offset unique to you, and the App publishes the distance of the shorter path; a route with too little left outside that area cannot be shared hidden. The offset is created once and kept in your own private iCloud database, so it is the same on all your devices; it is never published. The App remembers your last choice on your device. In every version from 1.16 the copy on your device is not changed. A challenge entry holds your display name and your progress total, and in versions 1.14 and 1.15 also the device identifier described below. For a step challenge, in versions 1.14 and 1.15 the total is the step count from Apple Health since the challenge began; from version 1.16 it is the steps of the walks and runs you recorded in Wockett since then (the step count Wockett recorded during each one, or an estimate from its distance), and rides are not counted. For a distance or pace challenge the total is worked out from the walks you recorded in Wockett. Nothing is published automatically — every share is an explicit action. You can report or block content from other users, and community text is filtered before publishing. Your live location is never part of community content. Apart from the routes, challenge totals and achievements you choose to share, nothing from your walk history or Apple Health is published.
Trail reports (optional, from version 1.17) Tell other walkers what a trail is like, from the answers of people who walked it From version 1.17, after a hike or a walk on a trail, the App may ask a few optional questions about that trail: whether dogs are allowed and on what terms, whether it suits a stroller or a wheelchair, what the surface is like, and a thumbs up or thumbs down for whether you liked it. Nothing is sent unless you tap an answer, and you can turn the questions off in Settings. Each answer is sent as a trail report holding the trail (its identifier in Wockett's trail data and its region), the question and your answer. A report holds no name, no free text and no location, and your walk is not sent with it. We count at most one answer to each question for each trail from an iCloud account (the newest). Reports are stored in Apple's public CloudKit database as private records that only Wockett's moderators can read, and, as with all community content, CloudKit records the anonymous identifier described under Community Content and Moderation as each report's creator. Because each report names a trail, our moderators can see which trails an account sent reports about; we use reports only to work out the counts below, to deal with abuse, and to delete reports when asked. Other users never see a report, who sent it, or how any one person answered. For a trail and a question, a moderator may publish only the most common answer and the number of walkers who gave it, and only once at least three walkers gave the same answer and at least two in three of those who answered agree. That published count holds no identifiers, and it stays until we withdraw it or the trail's data changes. Thumbs up and thumbs down answers are collected and counted by our moderators but are not shown in the App. We delete trail reports when they are about 12 months old. Delete my community data in the App does not remove them, because only our moderators can read them: email support@wockett.app to have them removed, and the App adds to that email the anonymous identifier we need to find them.
Trail nominations (optional) Let us feature trails walkers recommend, and, from version 1.17, tell you when one you nominated is featured When you nominate a trail, the App sends the trail (its identifier in Wockett's trail data, its name, its region, one point on it and its length) and any note you write; nothing about your location or your walks. A nomination is stored in Apple's public CloudKit database as a private record that only Wockett's moderators can read, and, as with all community content, CloudKit records the anonymous identifier described under Community Content and Moderation as its creator, so our moderators can see which trails an account nominated. From version 1.17, each nomination also carries a random code made on your iPhone, readable only by our moderators like the rest of the nomination. The App keeps that code, and the list of trails you nominated, on that device and in its device backups; it is not synced through iCloud, and deleting the App deletes it unless the device is restored from a backup. If we feature a trail you nominated, we publish a status record that anyone can read, holding only that the trail was featured, the trail's identifier and name, and the date. The record's name is worked out from the code, and it holds neither the code nor any identifier of you, so only the device that sent the nomination can tell which status is its own. The App looks for its status records about once a day and when you open My contributions, and when it finds one it shows it there and once on the Trails list; if it finds one while running in the background, it also shows one notification created on your device. Wockett does not send push notifications. If we withdraw a feature, we remove its status records as well; a feature that simply reached its end date keeps them, because the trail was featured.
Device identifier (challenges) Recognise your own entry on a challenge leaderboard (versions 1.14 and 1.15) In versions 1.14 and 1.15, the first time the App needs one for challenges, it creates a random identifier and keeps it on this device. It is not your device's serial number or an advertising identifier, and it does not contain your name. It is saved with every challenge entry you publish from this device, so it is stored in Apple's public CloudKit database with the entry and can be read with it there, although the App does not display it. The same identifier appears on all of this device's entries. From version 1.16, the App no longer creates this identifier or saves it with new entries; it recognises your own entry by the anonymous identifier CloudKit records as its creator (see Community content). When you next sync an entry published by an earlier version, the App removes the identifier from it; an entry you do not sync again keeps it until the entry is deleted.
Motion & Fitness Count your steps with your iPhone's motion sensor; during a walk, run or ride, check that you are still on foot rather than in a vehicle, and notice when you start moving again after an automatic pause; and, if you turn on the untracked walk nudge, look back over the day's motion activity to spot a walk you did not record Read on your device only. Step counts become part of the walks saved in your history (see Walk history). Motion data is not sent to Scoops Dev or any third party.
Calendar Delete walk reminders that versions 1.7 to 1.10 of the App added to your calendar, when you ask to delete one The App asks for calendar access only when you delete one of those old reminders. It looks up the events it created by their identifiers and deletes them; it does not store or send your calendar. Wockett no longer creates calendar events: walk reminders are now local notifications.

You can revoke any of these permissions at any time in iOS Settings → Wockett.

Data We Do Not Collect

  • We do not collect your name, email address, or any contact information
  • We do not create or require user accounts
  • We do not use analytics or crash-reporting SDKs
  • We do not use advertising or tracking frameworks
  • We do not collect photos, video, or camera data
  • We do not operate any servers of our own — on-device storage, sync, and community content all use Apple's iCloud/CloudKit infrastructure, governed by Apple's Privacy Policy

Third-Party Services

Wockett uses the following Apple platform services, governed by Apple's Privacy Policy, and one non-Apple service, listed last:

  • Apple Maps (MapKit) — used for route generation, on-screen directions, place search and nearby places, and directions to trails. Coordinate data is sent to Apple's servers to power these features.
  • Apple WeatherKit — provides weather conditions for your current location or for a point on a route (see Weather above).
  • Apple HealthKit — the Health data listed above is read from, and completed workouts are written to, your Health app. This data is not shared with any third party.
  • Apple iCloud / CloudKit — syncs your walk history, badges, and streaks between your own devices through your private iCloud database, and stores community content you choose to publish in a shared public database. Scoops Dev cannot read your private database.
  • OpenTopoData (opentopodata.org) — provides the elevation chart shown for a selected route. When you pick a route, up to 100 points along its path are sent to OpenTopoData over an encrypted connection to look up ground elevation. No account, identifier, name, or other data is sent with them, and the App does not use a key or token that could tie requests to you. This is the only non-Apple service the App talks to.

In-App Purchases

Optional tips are processed by Apple through the App Store. Tips do not unlock features, and there is nothing else to buy in the App. We never see your payment details, name or Apple ID; the App only asks the App Store on your device which purchases you have made.

Community Content and Moderation

Content you publish is stored in Apple's public CloudKit database. CloudKit records with each item the anonymous identifier Apple assigns to your iCloud account for Wockett, as its creator. Scoops Dev can view and remove published community content, but cannot see your private data. In versions 1.14 and 1.15 a shared route can show where you started and finished, so consider that before sharing a route that begins at home. From version 1.16 a shared route still shows where it starts and finishes unless you turn on Hide start and finish, which hides them inside a privacy area of at least 200 metres whose centre is moved by a private random offset unique to you. That hides the exact start and finish but can still show the area around them.

Blocking. When you block a user, that choice is kept on your device and hides their content from you; it is not sent to us. In version 1.14 the App recognises a blocked user by their display name; from version 1.15 it uses the anonymous identifier described above. In versions 1.14 and 1.15 your blocks are kept only on that device, so they are lost if you delete the App. From version 1.16 your block list is also kept in your own private iCloud database, so it stays after a reinstall and is the same on every device signed in to your iCloud account; like the rest of your private iCloud data, Scoops Dev cannot read it. You can see and undo your blocks in Settings > Community > Blocked people.

Reporting. When you report a post, route or challenge, it is hidden from you on your device. In version 1.14 a report is not sent to us; to bring content to our attention, email support@wockett.app. From version 1.15, the App also opens an email to support@wockett.app holding the item, its author's display name and its content, which you review and send yourself. From version 1.16, a report is instead sent to Scoops Dev as a private record in Apple's public CloudKit database that only Wockett's moderators can read. That report holds the item, its author's display name, a short excerpt of it, the reason you chose and any note you wrote; your name is not included, and CloudKit records only the anonymous identifier described above as its creator. If it cannot be sent, the App offers to email it to support@wockett.app instead.

Removing your content. Email support@wockett.app to have content you published removed. From version 1.16, you can also delete your own routes, feed posts and challenge entries in the App at any time (long-press one and choose Delete from Community), or delete all of your community content at once in Settings > Community > Delete my community data. That removes your shared routes, feed posts, challenge entries, challenges no one else has joined, your likes and Wocketts, and your community name. A challenge other people have joined is not deleted, because their entries would go with it; the App asks us to remove it instead. Reports and trail nominations you sent, and from version 1.17 trail reports, cannot be deleted in the App, because only our moderators can read them; email us to have them removed, and the App adds to that email the anonymous identifier we need to find them. Deleting community content does not touch your walk history or other private data.

Moderation records. When a Wockett moderator removes an item, a copy of it (its text and other fields, shortened if long) is kept with the reason for the decision in a record that only moderators can read, so the decision can be reviewed later.

Children's Privacy

Wockett is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the App, please contact us and we will take steps to address it.
